# rationale-voice

Write or edit a first-person rationale beat so it reads as Paul on his best day.
This is Paul's natural voice, tuned with the transferable half of Anthony Bourdain
(his reverence for people doing hard, unglamorous work) and stripped of the
swagger, cynicism, and vulgarity that are not Paul. It is not a Bourdain
impression; a famous-voice costume fails the same authenticity test as AI copy.

Full profile and calibration: `docs/rationale-voice.md`. This file is the
operational summary.

## Framing device: reverence

Frame every beat as service to the people who did the hard work. Name them, honor
their judgment, credit the mission: the analyst under pressure, the commander who
makes the call, the underfunded public servant who rarely gets a tool built for
them. Start from who it was for and what it cost them. Reverence is the organizing
principle, not decoration. Protect it above all.

## Keep (Paul's fingerprints)

- Gratitude-forward; getting to do the work reads as a privilege.
- Reverence for the underserved user, named.
- Plain-words reflex: the human version of the jargon.
- Folk concreteness ("wheat and chaff", "waterways people sail and play on").
- First person, plainly owned; end on what it meant for a real person.

## Tune (recurring tics)

- Cut intensifiers: really / very / so / a little bit.
- Trade abstract tricolons for one concrete image.
- Vary rhythm; a three-word sentence after a long one.
- Close on an image, not a feeling-word.

## Guardrails

- No vulgarity. No em-dashes (repo lint:prose hard-fails). No banned words (craft, seamless).
- Every claim true and sourceable; voice never buys an exaggeration.
- Register-fit: first-person beats only (bet, reflection, designer's note). Never captions, never the hero.
- Sincerity is a feature; do not make it cooler than Paul is.

## Calibration

Keep as-is: "Keep the wheat and remove the chaff." / "Good intel analysts don't trust data blindly."

Tune: "I felt really proud ... a little bit easier." becomes "These are public servants who rarely get a tool built for them. Mine was. That's the part I'm proud of."

## Apply

1. Start from reverence: who did this serve, what did it cost them?
2. Plain words; keep the human version, drop the jargon.
3. Cut every really / very / so; swap one abstract list for one concrete image.
4. Break rhythm with a short sentence.
5. Close on the person or thing, not the feeling.
6. Run guardrails: no vulgarity, no em-dash, no banned words, sourceable claims, right register.
