<section className="cs-section cs-section--frictions">

## Good intel analysts do not trust data blindly.

<p className="section-lede">I interviewed over a dozen people and watched them work, from junior analysts to flag officers. By doctrine, analysts weigh what they get, debate it, and make their own call.</p>

<FrictionMoment work="vermeer-woman-holding-balance" />

The assessments Michelle relied on were articles from other intelligence agencies: finished answers she could not question, and never live. When two disagreed, she had no way to know which was right, so she hesitated. In peacetime, that is caution. In war, the delay is a failure in itself.

</section>

<section className="cs-section cs-section--turn">

## How do you trust what you cannot question?

<p className="section-lede turn-answer">You don't. <span className="turn-answer__key">You make it show its work.</span></p>

The product team wanted the model to do more on its own, like kicking off workflows. I brought the research back, argued for a verification gate instead, and the team built it in.

Michelle works every flag through the gate before it goes up the chain.

</section>

<section className="cs-section">

## I designed the gate so the model brings the evidence and Michelle makes the call.

The evidence arrives live, while there is still time to act on it. A flag opens as a case, and the case holds everything she needs to defend her call, in the order she works it.

<GateWalkBento />

</section>
