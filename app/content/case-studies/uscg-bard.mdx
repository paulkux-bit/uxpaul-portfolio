<div className="eyebrow">The problem</div>

## Florida's data coordinator moves cases, not forms.

<p className="section-lede">She runs boating-incident reporting for the highest-volume jurisdiction in the country. Fifteen to thirty cases a session, several sessions a week.</p>

The legacy system fights her on every one: cases look complete and bounce, rejection emails name the case but never the field, and a sixty-day federal clock never stops running.

<PopUp anchor="courtroom">
Florida law enforcement uses BARD summary sheets in depositions. When what the officer entered doesn't match what the sheet prints, they testify about a software quirk instead of the incident.
</PopUp>

<div className="eyebrow">Three frictions</div>

## The real work was happening around the system, not in it.

I talked to twenty people who do the work, from the busiest states to the smallest territories.

<PopUp anchor="hook">
The first screen-share, the coordinator pulled up the same field across two state submissions. They didn't match. The redesign question changed in that minute.
</PopUp>

<PopUp anchor="research">
The all-56-states rule arrived screen-share by screen-share, until I reached for an unexpected reference: Facebook's "View As." The form needed the same move: let each side read the same record its own way, neither forced to adapt.
</PopUp>
