# Every recreational boating fatality in the United States is counted in one system.

After twenty years, the system that counts them had stopped serving the people who feed it.

The Boating Accident Report Database is federal infrastructure. Fifty-six jurisdictions file into it. The numbers it produces set safety policy, award grants, and defend federal rulemaking. I led its first ground-up replacement in two decades.

<div className="eyebrow">The research</div>

## When users build software to work around your software, the verdict is already in.

Twenty interviews across every volume tier — Florida to the territories. Mostly remote. The screen-shares were the unlock.

Watching people work, I kept seeing the same thing: a dozen tabs open. Everyone was reconciling the same record across views the system refused to reconcile for them.

Then I looked at the Coast Guard side. Reviewers had built their own tools to make the legacy readable. The case for rebuilding had already been written, in shadow IT.

<div className="eyebrow">The reframe</div>

## How might we let one record carry two readings?

That question shaped everything that followed.

<div className="eyebrow">The principle</div>

## Say one thing loudly. Say everything else quietly.

Because the person reading any surface is on their fortieth case of the week, not their first. Evaluated against Nielsen's heuristics. Held to Section 508 and WCAG 2.2 AA, computed mathematically across every pairing.

### The Insight Engine

The legacy dashboard was tables. Nothing surfaced a signal.

I built a system that reads the normalized data and writes one plain-language insight per surface. The same engine that writes one terse line on the dashboard writes a headline on the annual report.

The decision I'm most sure about: no AI. Once the forms were normalized, every insight was deterministic. In 2026 the reflex is to bolt an LLM onto everything. This needed the opposite. The power wasn't a model; it was data quality.
</PopUp>

### The form

One record, two readings. *Your View* for filing. *USCG View* for reviewing. The toggle is the all-56-states rule made interactive: if any state populates a field in real submissions, the field stays. Verified against actual sample submissions from Illinois, Utah, and California.

### The system

Three-layer tokens for a twenty-five-year horizon. Status and sentiment kept parallel, not nested, so either can diverge without a refactor. Eight workflow states, each with their own hue, so color alone tells a reviewer who holds the case.

<div className="eyebrow">Reflection</div>

## What I got wrong.

I assumed a lean form was a better form. On the People form alone, eighteen fields looked dead by every measure I had. Then I checked them against real state submissions — Illinois, Utah, California. The "dead" fields were populated.

<blockquote className="pull-quote pull-quote--quiet">
  <p>A wireframe shows render state, not field inventory.</p>
</blockquote>

That reversal became the governing rule: one master form, and if any single state uses a field, it stays.

<PopUp anchor="reflection">
I tuned the entire Insight Engine voice to one persona. The analyst and executive surfaces don't exist yet. So I don't yet know whether the voice I built belongs to the user or to the system. I'll find out when those surfaces ship.
</PopUp>
