**Boating accident cases are rising**, and each one becomes a federal record, filed against a statutory deadline.

**This isn't just paperwork.** The record is Maria's. It can end up in court.

**The old system made required data easy to skip.** Her cases came back from the Coast Guard for corrections she could have caught at entry.

## How do you unify fifty-six jurisdictions without changing one?

<p className="section-lede">Standardize the data, not the people.</p>

{/* ANTITHESIS BUDGET: BARD keeps two "X, not Y" lines, this lede and section 2's heading.
    The house budget is one. Both were kept on purpose: section 2's states what the research
    found, this one states what I did about it. The others were cut, section 1's "not easier"
    and section 8's "not design". Do not add a third. */}

**They were never all going to agree on one form.** I left local processes alone and built a standard data structure underneath. A crash in Hawaii and a crash in Florida can finally be counted the same way.

</section>

<section className="cs-section">

## Each state files a different form into the same record.

<p className="section-lede">Federal fields are required. Everything else is up to the jurisdiction.</p>

**The modular form gives two people their own screen.** Maria sees only the fields her case needs. The Coast Guard opens that record in its own view, with a compliance score that fills in as she types.
