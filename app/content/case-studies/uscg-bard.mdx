<div className="eyebrow">Why the old system failed</div>

## Investigators need to get every case right, but the system made that harder, not easier.

**Boating accident cases are rising**, and each one becomes a federal record, filed against a statutory deadline.

**This isn't just paperwork.** Any case could end up as evidence.

**The old system made required data easy to skip**, so cases went out breaking federal compliance rules.

<section className="cs-section">

<div className="eyebrow">My approach</div>

## How do you unify fifty-six jurisdictions without changing one?

**Standardize the data, not the people.** With fifty-six different forms, no one could see trends or act on them.

People kept trying to force a single national form, but it never worked. I took a different approach: keep everyone's process, just standardize the data underneath.

</section>

<section className="cs-section">

<div className="eyebrow">How I solved it</div>

## Flexible forms, unified records.

All jurisdictions share a single data structure. Federal fields are required; everything else is optional. Investigators see only what their case needs, with compliance scored in real time. The Coast Guard reviews the same record from its own view.

<section className="cs-section">

<div className="eyebrow">Building the system</div>

## The hardest part was collaboration, not design.

<p className="section-lede">Standardizing the forms was a technical challenge; getting fifty-six jurisdictions to buy in was the real work.</p>

**I thought the win was a shorter form, fewer fields. It turned out every state had reasons for the ones it kept.** So I kept them all, made them optional, and built a common foundation underneath. Nobody lost a field. Everyone got the same base.
