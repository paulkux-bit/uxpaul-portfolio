# Paul's design-rationale voice

The voice for the first-person, "you speak" beats of a uxpaul.com case study: the
bet, the reflection, an optional designer's note. Distilled from two raw samples
Paul wrote about FDT-E and Bard. It is Paul's natural voice, tuned with the
transferable half of Anthony Bourdain's craft (his reverence for people who do
hard, unglamorous work) and stripped of the parts that are not Paul: the swagger,
the cynicism, the vulgarity.

Not a costume. The goal is Paul on his best day, not a Bourdain impression. A
famous-voice impression fails the same authenticity test as AI copy.

## The framing device: reverence

Every rationale beat is framed as service to the people who do the hard work.
Name them. Honor their judgment. Credit the mission. Paul reveres the intel
analyst under pressure, the commander who makes the call, the underfunded public
servant who rarely gets a tool built for them. Start from who the work was for and
what it cost them, and the rationale writes itself. Reverence is the organizing
principle, not decoration.

This is Bourdain's respect for line cooks and dishwashers, pointed at Paul's
users. It is the single most distinctive and most authentic thing in his writing.
Protect it above everything else here.

## Keep: Paul's natural fingerprints

- Gratitude-forward. He frames getting to do the work as a privilege. Let that open or close a beat.
- Reverence for the underserved user, named and honored (the analyst, the CO, the public servant).
- The plain-words reflex. He translates jargon to the human version by instinct. Trust it.
- Folk concreteness: "keep the wheat and remove the chaff", "rivers, lakes and waterways people sail and play on".
- First person, plainly owned: "my job was", "the biggest problem I found". No hedging.
- End on what it meant for a real person.

## Tune: the recurring tics

- Cut intensifiers. really / very / so / a little bit pile up in both samples. Delete them; let the concrete noun carry the weight. "Humbling" beats "really humbling".
- Trade abstract tricolons for one concrete thing. "position, arrangement and distribution" becomes the actual thing on the screen. The vivid list stays; the abstract one goes.
- Vary the rhythm. After a long sentence, a very short one. Three words is a sentence.
- Land the close on an image, not a feeling-word. Show the thing you are proud of instead of naming the pride.

## Guardrails (non-negotiable)

- No vulgarity. No em-dashes (the repo's lint:prose hard-fails on U+2014). No banned words (craft, seamless).
- Cross-check discipline: no claim that is not true and sourceable. Voice never buys an exaggeration.
- Register-fit. This voice is only for the first-person beats: the bet, the reflection, a designer's note. Never the captions (terse evidence register). Never the hero (restraint-led, names-led). It is seasoning, used where Paul already speaks as himself.
- Sincerity is a feature. Do not make it cooler, edgier, or more detached than Paul is. He is warm and earnest, not sardonic.

## Calibration (from Paul's own words)

Already the voice, keep as-is:
- "Keep the wheat and remove the chaff."
- "Good intel analysts don't trust data blindly."
- "Public servants who don't get useful and beautiful projects."

Tune in action:
- Raw: "I felt really proud working on this project because it was helping the stressed and overworked public servant get their jobs done a little bit easier."
- Tuned: "These are public servants who rarely get a tool built for them. Mine was. That's the part I'm proud of."
- Raw: "The amount of pressure that users go through evaluating information and then making a judgement call is really humbling."
- Tuned: "An analyst stares at a picture that won't agree with itself and has to make the call. That is the most humbling thing I have designed for."

## Apply: the checklist for a rationale beat

1. Start from reverence: who did this serve, and what did it cost them?
2. Say it in plain words. If a sentence has jargon, write the human version and keep that one.
3. Cut every really / very / so. Replace one abstract list with one concrete image.
4. Break the rhythm with a short sentence.
5. Close on the person or the thing, not the feeling-word.
6. Run the guardrails: no vulgarity, no em-dash, no banned words, every claim sourceable, right register.
