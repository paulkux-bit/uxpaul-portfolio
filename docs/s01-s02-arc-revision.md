# §01 and §02, revised against the two-premise arc

Drafted 26 Aug 2026 from Paul's section-by-section reading. **§01 changes shape;
§02 gains the sentence §01 gives up.** Nothing else moves.

---

## The problem this fixes

There were **two** wrong premises, not one, and the shipped §01 states them both
without committing to either:

- the **heading** answers Paul's premise — *speed*
- the **lede** states leadership's premise — *inventory*

They also die to different evidence. **The quotes kill Paul's** — neither voice
mentions how long anything took. **The data kills leadership's** — the misses
concentrate where URBN charged most, which is a promise problem rather than a
stock problem, and that argument lives in §02 and §03.

And the research method is currently described **twice**, once in each section's
lede.

## §01 — as shipped

```mdx
## Speed was the wrong thing to measure.

<p className="section-lede">Leadership thought this was an inventory problem. I put our researcher into the customer feedback and the help tickets, then ran what she found against the order data.</p>

<TestimonyPair />
```

## §01 — proposed

```mdx
## Speed was the wrong thing to measure.

<p className="section-lede">Leadership thought this was an inventory problem. I thought it was a speed problem, and that we had to go win a two-day race against Prime.</p>

<TestimonyPair />

**Shoppers could tell you whether it came when we said it would.** Not how long it took. My premise was gone. Leadership's was still standing.
```

**Lede: 26 words** (was 28). **Closer: 23** (was 22). Prose total **49**, was 50.

### What changed and why

**The lede now states both premises and resolves neither.** The heading has
already said one of them is wrong, so naming his own belief immediately under it
is a confession rather than a setup. That is the *"what failed"* hiring question,
which the house rules are explicit means the designer's own failure and not a
business one — and it now lands in the first section instead of §06.

**The research method is gone from §01.** It belongs in §02, where it already is.
A reader meets the quotes labelled `RESEARCH SESSION` and `APP STORE REVIEW`
without being told a study happened, and infers it — which is showing rather than
telling, and it lets the evidence arrive before the methodology.

**The closer now hands off.** *"My premise was gone. Leadership's was still
standing"* is what makes §02 and §03 necessary rather than merely next. It is
also precise about an asymmetry the interview record establishes: **Paul was
wrong; leadership was incomplete.** The record's line is *"Leadership had half
the diagnosis. Inventory was a real problem, but the research showed the
compounding effect."* Half right is not the same as wrong, and the draft does not
flatten them.

**"Not how long it took" is new and it is doing the work the heading cannot.**
The heading says speed was the wrong measure; this says what the right one was,
in the same breath, without repeating the heading's words.

## §02 — as shipped

```mdx
## Her order broke in three places.

<p className="section-lede">A researcher ran the interviews and I worked the analysis with her, against the order data and the customer service queue.</p>
```

## §02 — proposed

```mdx
## Her order broke in three places.

<p className="section-lede">A researcher ran the interviews. I worked the analysis with her, against the order data and the customer service queue. The same three breaks kept coming back.</p>
```

**Lede: 29 words** (was 21).

### What changed and why

**Split into two sentences.** The attribution rule from the interview record is
strict — *a sole researcher owned the primary research; never write "I
interviewed customers."* The shipped version honours it in one clause; splitting
it makes the division of labour unmissable rather than something a fast reader
runs past.

**The third sentence is new and it sets up the beats.** *"The same three breaks
kept coming back"* tells the reader what three things are about to appear and
why there are three of them. The shipped lede describes a method and then drops
the reader into an unannounced list. Supported by the record: the 2019 deck
structured this problem in three parts twice, independently.

## §03 — not drafted, but Paul's reading changes it

Recorded because it resolves an open question and opens a new one.

**It closes the adjacency finding.** `JourneyLine` and `InversionChart` sitting
adjacent was flagged as a possible house-rules §8 repeat. Under this arc they are
not a repeat: **`JourneyLine` is breadth** — the whole journey is affected —
**`InversionChart` is depth** — and it is worst where we charged most. Two halves
of one argument, justified on axis. That is §8-legal, **provided the section is
written so prose sits between them** rather than stacking the two drawings.

**It opens a provenance question.** Paul's reading has §03 landing on *sentiment
points to trust being broken*. The trust framing —
*"delivery promise is not a logistics feature, it is a trust system"* — sits in
the interview record under **Outcome**, not under anything attributed as a 2019
quote. **Before a section closes on it, confirm it is Paul's line from the time
rather than a later summary.** A study this careful about quotation should not
put a reconstructed sentence in its most quotable position.
